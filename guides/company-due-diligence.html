Hong Kong Company Due Diligence: A Research Checklist

Hong Kong · Practical guide · Updated November 2026

Company due diligence is a process of testing information, not merely collecting it. A registry search can establish useful corporate facts, but it cannot answer every question about ownership, financial condition, reputation or transaction risk.

Confirm the legal entity

Start with the exact legal name and company number. Check that contracts, invoices, websites and payment instructions refer to the same entity. Be alert to similarly named businesses and trading names.

Review status and filing history

Check available registry information, filing dates and relevant changes. Interpret status in context and as at the date shown. Verify current status officially when a decision depends on it.

Understand ownership and officers

Review available director and shareholder information, paying attention to dates and the legal meaning of each field. Shareholder information may not provide a complete picture of ultimate beneficial ownership or control.

Check wider risk factors

Depending on the purpose and risk level, consider litigation, insolvency, sanctions, regulatory actions and adverse media. Resolve potential identity matches before treating them as confirmed findings.

Key point: Document evidence and limitations
