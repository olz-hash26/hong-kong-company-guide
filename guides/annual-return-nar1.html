Hong Kong Annual Return (NAR1): A Practical Guide

Hong Kong · Practical guide · Updated November 2026

The NAR1 is commonly associated with the annual return of a Hong Kong local company limited by shares. An annual return records prescribed particulars at a point in time; it is not a complete account of the company’s operations or financial condition.

What an annual return records

The annual return helps maintain the statutory company record. Depending on the company and form, it may contain particulars about the registered office, share capital, shareholders, directors and company secretary.

Check the return date and deadline

The relevant return date and filing deadline depend on the company’s circumstances and statutory rules. Do not assume every company has the same deadline. Consult current Companies Registry guidance.

Read it as a dated snapshot

A return describes particulars at its relevant date and may not reflect later changes. For a current picture, compare it with subsequent filings and current registry information.

Avoid over-interpreting the document

A filed annual return does not by itself prove that a company is trading, solvent, reputable or free from litigation. It is one source in a broader review.

Key point: Use current Companies Registry guidance for filing obligations and deadlines. In research, note the return date and compare it with later records.
